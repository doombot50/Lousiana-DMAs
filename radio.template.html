A companion to Louisiana Airwaves

Louisiana Dial

Television money follows population. Radio money follows audience — and that makes a radio ledger the closest thing in the public record to a campaign admitting who it was talking to. Louisiana committees have put $11.7M into the state's radio stations since 2000, a tenth of what they spent on TV. Where it went says more.

Every radio dollar, by station format

The dial

A third of it buys Black radio

Group the stations by what they broadcast and one bucket dominates: urban, gospel and Black talk formats take 33% of all radio spending — nearly double news/talk, the next largest. On television no comparable split exists, because a TV station sells one undifferentiated audience to everybody. Radio sells specific audiences, and Louisiana campaigns have spent a quarter-century buying one in particular.

“Black-audience” groups the formats the radio industry itself markets as reaching Black listeners — urban contemporary, urban adult contemporary, gospel, and brokered Black talk. It is a description of programming and of how airtime is sold, not of any station's or listener's politics.

The fingerprint

Every campaign's radio buy is a targeting statement

Break each committee's spending down by format and the strategy is legible without a single poll. John Bel Edwards put 61% of his radio money into Black-audience stations; Kathleen Blanco 74%; Walter Boasso 99%. Jeff Landry put 48% into news/talk, and the conservative PACs split roughly 40/40 between talk and country. The exception proves the rule: Bobby Jindal bought all of it — 26% country, 25% Black-audience, 20% talk — the radio signature of a candidate assembling a landslide rather than a base.

Retail politics

The cheapest airtime in Louisiana

For the median station here, a buy runs — against $4,221 on television. At the bottom of the scale sits something television has no equivalent of: KVPI in Ville Platte, a classic-country-and-Cajun-French AM station, has taken money from 61 different committees at an average of $244 a spot. Sheriffs, assessors, school-board candidates and constables buy this radio. It is the layer of Louisiana campaigning that never shows up in a television ledger at all.

Ownership

Different companies than television entirely

The Gray-and-Nexstar duopoly that collects 78% of Louisiana's political TV money has no presence here. Radio's money goes to iHeartMedia, Townsquare, Cumulus and Audacy — and, unlike television, a substantial share stays with locally owned stations that answer to nobody's national programming department.

Method & sources

Where this comes from

Built by build_radio_spend.py from Louisiana Ethics Administration expenditure rows, 2000–2026 — the same filings behind Louisiana Airwaves. Radio needs a stricter matcher than television did, for one reason:

Four-letter call signs collide with English

Radio has ten times as many call signs as television, and a plain pattern match over these filings returns WINE (Total Wine), KING (Smoothie King Center), WORD (Last Word Strategies), WEST (Ryan West) and KYLE (Kyle Ardoin) as radio stations — of phantom spending. So nothing is matched on shape alone: a row counts only if its call sign is in a hand-verified station reference, or carries an explicit -AM/-FM marker. Rows failing that test are excluded and the amount is reported in the output.

What is and isn't classified

of the dollars here sit on the 66 stations whose format, city and owner were verified individually against published sources. The remainder — across roughly smaller call signs — is identifiable as radio but not yet classified, so it appears as its own bucket rather than being dropped or guessed into a category. The largest single station in that tail is about $55K. Format shares are quoted against the full total, so the unclassified tail dilutes them rather than disappearing from the denominator.

Two overlaps with the TV page

WWL and KTAL share call signs between a television and a radio station. The Airwaves page counts their bare-call rows as television; this page counts only rows explicitly marked radio (WWL-AM, KTAL-FM), so no dollar is claimed twice. That radio-marked total is .

Geography, deliberately not the spine

Radio does not use the DMAs the TV page is built on. Nielsen Audio rates only a handful of Louisiana metros, and much of this money goes to stations in unrated territory — Mamou, Crowley, Berwick, Ville Platte, Moreauville. A parish map would leave a large share of the spending homeless, which is why format, not place, organizes this page. City of licence is in the ledger for anyone who wants it.
