How this is built

Where the numbers come from

Which groups. Every filer the Ethics Board registers as a political committee rather than a candidate that spent at least $300,000 in the 2019 or 2023 cycle, or $100,000 so far in 2027. A cycle is the four years ending in a governor's race. Spending and raising are each group's cycle totals from the campaign-finance dashboard.

The split. Each payment is sorted by who received it: a Louisiana candidate or committee (or a federal, party or out-of-state campaign) counts as backing; a TV or radio station, cable or digital ad seller, or media-buying firm counts as airtime; everything else is the rest of running a campaign. Payments a filing leaves unnamed fall in the last group. The split covers 2000–2026; the totals come from the dashboard.

Kind. Air war: 40% or more of its biggest recent cycle went to airtime. Giver: half or more went to candidates and committees. Party & caucus: by name. Mixed: neither.

Backed and hired. The candidates and committees that received the most from a group in the cycle shown, and the firms it paid the most. People paid as individuals are never listed by name, and banks, hotels, taxes and fees are left off the hired list (their dollars still count). TV and radio come from the same station matching as the television and radio pages. Party is the filing's own label, corrected only where a publicly aligned committee files as OTH; most PACs carry no party.

Spending is what each committee reported in its Louisiana Ethics Administration filings, nominal dollars. Built by build_players_page.py. See also vendor factions, which firms work for whom.
