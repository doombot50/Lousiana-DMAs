Louisiana Airwaves

Louisiana political committees have paid the state's television stations $121 million since 2000. This is where that money went: which of the seven Nielsen markets it bought, which stations banked it, and — after a year of consolidation — which companies now collect it. The surprise is that the biggest station group by count is not the biggest by revenue.

The pulse

Louisiana buys television every four years

Plotted by year, the money has a heartbeat: the state's governor's races — 2003, 2007, 2011, 2015, 2019, 2023 — account for most of every dollar ever paid to these stations. The 2019 Edwards–Rispone runoff alone moved $26.4M, more than the nineteen quietest years combined.

Ownership

More stations, less money

Twelve months of deals redrew this ledger. Nexstar owns or operates thirteen of the thirty stations — the most of any group — but collects 30.4% of the money. Gray Media holds ten and collects 47.6%, because its ten include the news-leading CBS or NBC affiliate in six of seven markets. Station count measures reach; revenue measures which newscast the ads actually run inside.

The buyers

Who paid for the air war

Two governors dominate the whole century of filings. John Bel Edwards spent $11.3M on television across 29 of the 30 stations; Bobby Jindal spent $8.57M. Behind them sit the outside groups that fought the 2019 and 2023 races — the RGA's Right Direction PAC, Gumbo PAC, and The Fund for Louisiana's Future — each buying between 23 and 27 of the 30 stations.

Committee spending across all 30 stations. Party is the filing's own label, except that Louisiana files most PACs and some candidates as OTH, which says nothing about which side bought the airtime. Where a filer's alignment is public record the label is corrected — the RGA's PAC to Republican; Gumbo PAC, Walter Boasso and Buddy Leach to Democratic; Jay Dardenne to Republican. A filing that states a party is left alone unless public reporting shows it is wrong: Blair Downing Edwards, a Republican 2026 Supreme Court candidate whose committee files as Democratic, is shown as Republican. Every correction is listed in filings.py.

Method & sources

Where this comes from

Dollar figures are computed by build_station_spend.py in this repo, which reads 1.51 million expenditure rows from the Louisiana Ethics Administration filings (via the campaign-finance project's .la_cache) and sums what every committee paid each station. Three corrections matter:

Spelling variants. One station is filed 15–30 ways — KADN, KADN-TV, KADN - TV, KADN-FOX, KADN-TV CHANNEL 15. All fold to one call sign.

Radio sisters. WWL and KTAL share their call signs with radio stations. Rows explicitly marked radio (WWL-AM, WWL RADIO, KTAL-FM) are excluded — in total. Rows carrying only the bare call sign cannot be resolved either way; they are counted as television, and that amount — , nearly all of it WWL — is disclosed here rather than buried. Treat WWL-TV's and KTAL's totals as ceilings.

Combined buys. sits in rows naming two co-owned stations at once (KARD-TV KTVE-TV, WGMB WVLA-TV); those amounts are split evenly rather than assigned to whichever sorts first.

One gap: the 2008 expenditure file is absent from the upstream data release, so 2008 is missing from every figure on this page. It was an off-cycle year — the off-years either side drew $992K in 2006 and $528K in 2009 — so the effect on the totals is small, but it is not zero.

Parish boundaries are U.S. Census cartographic files; parish-to-DMA assignment was computed by point-in-polygon against Nielsen DMA boundaries and cross-checked against published market lists. Ownership is as reported through September 2026.
