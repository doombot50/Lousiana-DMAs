A spin-off of Louisiana's vendor factions

Louisiana Airwaves

When a Louisiana campaign goes on television, the check lands in one of seven Nielsen markets. The vendor-factions graph found those checks in the expenditure filings — WDSU alone is paid by 97 of the state's 500 biggest-spending committees. This page is the geography underneath the buys: every station the committees pay, the market it broadcasts to, and — after a year of consolidation — who owns it.

The map

Seven markets, one air war

Nielsen slices the country into Designated Market Areas — the unit a TV ad buy actually purchases. Louisiana's parishes fall into seven, from New Orleans (№ 50 in the country) down to Alexandria (№ 183). A statewide race has to buy all seven; the two committees that fought the 2023 governor's race did exactly that. Click a market for its stations.

Ownership

Two companies own the air war

Twelve months of deals redrew this column of the ledger. Gray Media — already owner of the CBS or NBC leader in five markets — picked up KATC from Scripps and KADN/KLAF from Allen Media in 2026, and now has a station in all seven Louisiana markets. Nexstar owns or operates thirteen, counting the Tegna stations a federal injunction currently holds in limbo. What's left over: one Hearst station, three family-owned holdouts, and three independents.

MAR 19 2026Nexstar closed its $6.2B purchase of Tegna — WWL-TV and WUPL in New Orleans — then a preliminary injunction won by DirecTV and eight state attorneys general froze integration in April. WUPL's divestiture was reported pending. Marked *.

MAY 15 2026Gray and Scripps completed a five-market station swap; KATC Lafayette joined Gray.

MAY 2026Gray closed its $171M purchase of ten Allen Media stations, including KADN and KLAF-LD — giving Gray both Lafayette ABC and Fox.

The ledger

Every station, every buy

For each station: how many of Louisiana's 500 biggest-spending committees name it in an expenditure filing, 2000–2026. The pattern is the market pecking order made visible — committees buy the news-leading network affiliates first (WDSU, WWL, WBRZ, WAFB, KTBS), and the CW and low-power stations last.

Counts come from the vendor-factions build (top 500 committees by service spend; call-sign spelling variants merged). Where filings use a bare call sign shared with a radio sister — WWL, KTAL — a small share of the count may be radio buys.

The buyers

Who buys the whole state

Inside the vendor-factions graph, the committees sharing the most stations are the air-war heavyweights of the 2023 governor's race: Gumbo PAC (the anti-Landry super PAC) and the RGA's Right Direction PAC each bought at least 25 of the 30 stations — the same air war, flown from opposite directions.

Station counts are a floor, not a total — they count only buys visible on shared-vendor edges that survived the graph's thresholds.

Method & sources

Where this comes from

Spending data is the Louisiana Ethics Administration's campaign expenditure filings, as processed by the vendor-factions build (July 2026 run: top 500 committees by service spend, 2000–2026, with commodity vendors and political recipients filtered out). Station-level committee counts are that build's vendor document frequencies, with call-sign variants (KADN, KADN-TV, KADN - TV…) merged.

Parish boundaries are U.S. Census cartographic files; parish-to-DMA assignment was computed against Nielsen DMA boundary data and cross-checked against published market lists. Market ranks and TV-household counts are the 2024–25 Nielsen season. Ownership is as reported through mid-2026, including the Nexstar–Tegna close (and injunction), the Gray–Scripps swap, and the Gray–Allen Media deal.
